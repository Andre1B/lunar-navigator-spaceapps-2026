## Evidence and findings, ranked

Reviewed `docs/REDESIGN-V2.md`, `README.md`, scientific README summary, current stylesheet tokens, `HomeV2.jsx`, `InteractiveTour.jsx`, production screenshots `desktop-home.png` and `desktop-1440-simulator-overview-hud.png`, and the generated `lunar-redesign-concept-v2.png`. No new browser or performance checks executed. Historical release scores do not validate this redesign.

1. **The explorer gives its subject too little space.** In the supplied desktop screenshot, the Moon is approximately 250 px tall within a 1320 x 540 px scene; much of the scene is empty, and the point of interest is not visually explained. Increasing detail alone will not repair this. Camera framing, selected-point identification and immediate access to time are the first work.
2. **The entry question is obscured by terminology.** V1's “Find your window” is memorable but does not explain the tool. The HomeV2 draft improves this with three concrete questions; “Understand the landing” and “build a plan” still imply a broader capability than terrain visibility. Prefer “Explore lunar light. See what a delay changes.” Supporting text: “Compare sunlight and direct Earth visibility at four south-pole study points.”
3. **The generated concept fixes scale but adds competing decoration.** Its near terrain, named cameras and contiguous time strip are useful. Its cyan outlines on nearly every region, glowing buttons, oversized luminous pin, lens flare and large right-hand coaching card divide attention. None is required for a vivid, modern product. The detailed crater scene is also not a promise that the actual 40 m geometry can deliver that fidelity. The drawing's 2 m height is outside the supplied 1/5 m choices. Do not use its lighting, geography or scales as scientific specifications.
4. **The tutorial describes controls before producing an insight.** The draft goes through cameras, location and modes before time and observation. Ten readable steps can still feel like ten interruptions. Lead with one actual interaction and its interpretation, then progressively introduce other tools. The Free explanation should come after the baseline has made sense.
5. **A successful render is not a successful first impression.** Existing QA is strong technical evidence for v1, but the user rejected its appearance. Treat new visual acceptance and comprehension as pending until the new product is seen; never infer them from Lighthouse.

## Nine implementation decisions

1. **One clear invitation.** Above the first fold: concrete question, one sentence of scope, primary “Start guided mission · about 10 min”, secondary “Open workspace”. The human previously requested visible Faithful and Free entry buttons: preserve both explicitly, with short explanations, rather than hiding Free. Group them beneath the main invitation as mode choices or keep “Experiment in Free Mode” a lower-emphasis visible action. Guided entry defaults to Faithful. Do not make new visitors learn astronomy mode names to find the tutorial. Keep Learn, Compare, Evidence and Credits immediately available.

2. **Editorial home, working explorer.** Home gets generous whitespace, a large image and a short horizontal sequence: choose a point → change arrival → inspect evidence. Explorer gets a compact title, scene, setup and time as one tool. Avoid duplicating the entire header, an introductory hero and a second set of navigation cards inside the explorer. At 1440 x 900, the scene and the complete primary time control should be visible together without scrolling; this is a proposed composition target, not a measured pass.

3. **Vivid color with a job.** Proposed tokens: background `#090D14`, solid panel `#141C28`, divider `#344154`, primary text `#F5F7FA`, secondary `#B2BFD2`, action `#2E96F5`, Sun `#FFC978`, Earth `#55D4E8`, warning `#FFB36B`. Keep current text/muted/blue tokens where they already work. Use dark text on filled bright blue/gold buttons; check actual contrast in all states. Reserve cyan for Earth information, gold for Sun and focus, blue for actions, orange for artificial configuration. Never encode these distinctions by hue alone. No outer glows, gradient borders or translucent panels over terrain. A solid surface and one quiet dividing line are enough.

4. **Typography with hierarchy, not more fonts.** Keep the existing Overpass/Segoe UI stack; no new font download is necessary. Home headline 56–68 px desktop / 36–42 px phone, tight but comfortable line height around 1.05; short measure, no forced break on narrow screens. Page title 28–36 px, body 16–18 px at 1.5, control labels 14–16 px. Use monospace only for UTC, coordinates and numeric readouts; tabular numerals for changing values. Scientific limitations must remain readable, not relegated to tiny low-contrast footnotes. Proposed 8 px control radii, 12 px panels, 1 px dividers; chips are for state, not every navigation item.

5. **Camera composition must earn the new geometry.** A dedicated Moon overview should put the lunar diameter at roughly 65–80% of scene height, offset to leave breathing room for the selected-point label. Fit bodies separately in Moon & Earth; do not force the Moon-size target where physical separations make that impossible. South pole/local terrain uses an oblique camera so near ridges, middle relief and far silhouette establish depth. Frame the selected point clear of the setup/tour regions. Keep Sun direction, surface orientation and physical relief source-driven; if the selected instant is dark, explain that condition rather than painting light onto it. No promised Earth in every local frame. No auto-rotation on arrival. A true physical sky can be sparse or dark; that is an educational state, not a reason to fabricate scenery.

6. **Five views, one understandable question each.** Moon & Earth: spatial context. South pole: where the point is. Local terrain: what surrounds it (explicit 40 m display geometry). Surface sky: what is above the terrain horizon at 1/5 m. Solar context: wider spatial relationships. Use named buttons or an accessible compact switcher, keep the active camera obvious, and expose Reset view and keyboard controls. Camera changes do not change the computed visibility. Keep that distinction next to the scene, not solely in Evidence. Labels should be short, stable and occlusion-aware; no large map pin floating above relief without an actual anchor.

7. **Time belongs to the scene.** Attach Play/Pause, step, UTC and the scrubber immediately below it on the same solid base. Put Sun and Earth tracks directly under the scrubber with names and legends. Distinguish “fraction of disc visible” from a binary on/off signal. Keep a stable cursor and synchronized readout. On mobile, scene first, time second, setup in an inline expandable region; no bottom sheet covering the thing being learned. Retain the visible experimental warning while Free configuration is active, and never show its experiments as mission recommendations.

8. **Animation should reveal a change.** Proposed timing: 140–180 ms control state; 180–240 ms route entry with at most 6–8 px travel; 600–800 ms cancellable camera reposition. One coordinated camera motion per selection, not orbit plus zoom plus page motion. Time playback is user-initiated and sample semantics remain truthful; do not interpolate displayed metrics into false precision. Stop camera tween when the person drags. On reduced motion, apply cameras/routes immediately and retain explicit playback controls. Avoid perpetual star drift, pulsing pins, scroll parallax and repeated attention-seeking onboarding animations. Render on demand while idle.

9. **Teach an observation, then the model.** Tour order: purpose → choose point → move time/pause → read Sun and Earth tracks → switch local terrain/surface sky → compare delay → interpret darkness interval → inspect evidence → optional Free experiment. Each step has one action and one observable consequence, not a paragraph listing controls. Never promise a visible change at every arbitrary instant; choose a source-verified example or let the user inspect the track first. Keep Back/Next/Skip/close; no locked stages. On desktop, place coaching in reserved space outside the target; on phones, insert it in document flow or reserve space, rather than overlaying the controls. The current `top = min(innerHeight - 250, ...)` assumption does not establish that a long/zoomed tour card fits: verify its measured height, target accessibility and 200% zoom. Announce step changes, restore focus to a sensible originating control, and preserve scenario state across tour navigation.

## Bounded comprehension and review protocol

**First 20 seconds — proposal, not recorded evidence.** Show a fresh participant Home with no spoken explanation. After 20 seconds, ask: What does this help you compare? What would you click first? What changes when arrival is delayed? Record exact answers and first chosen action. Desired understanding: sunlight plus direct Earth visibility at lunar study points, an obvious guided/open entry, and changing time as the experiment. If they infer flight guidance or guaranteed communications, revise the wording. If they remember only “a 3D Moon”, revise the hierarchy. A small convenience sample is directional evidence, not a statistical success-rate claim; record participant count, viewport and whether prior knowledge was present. Never substitute an agent's reading for a human comprehension result.

**Three-minute review route — proposal, no competitive claim.** 0:00–0:20 understand question and scope; 0:20–1:10 change time and explain one source-derived visible result; 1:10–2:10 change arrival delay in Compare and inspect the original/delayed/alternative scenarios; 2:10–3:00 open Evidence and identify a source and a material limitation. Capture the exact build, inputs, screenshots and route completion. If a participant needs narration to find the next action, record where. Do not claim “judge ready”, award potential or superior design from this timed exercise.

**Release remains the existing gate.** Frontend owner runs build, tests, credits, production Lighthouse >=90, core workflow, keyboard, responsive and reduced-motion checks against the redesign. Scientific owner reviews geometry conventions. Visual review should inspect actual desktop and phone home/explorer/compare renders, a dark-time state, Free warning, tour collision and non-WebGL fallback. All are pending here. No extra blocking taste criteria are invented by this brief.
