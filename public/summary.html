Learn by doing.

The interactive guide explains the purpose, controls and limits through seven lessons: raise an observer, distinguish shadow from night, experiment with clocks and read visibility metrics. A ten-step tour points to the actual controls. Camera presets connect a real-scale lunar globe, the south pole, a surface sky and a 20 km local terrain window.

A comparison grounded in data.

Compare an original arrival, a delayed arrival and an alternative point. The prototype includes Connecting ridge, Shackleton rim, Nobile rim 1 and Malapert massif, with 1 m and 5 m observer heights, from 1 March to 28 April 2027. These published points of interest are not certified landing sites.

Inspect mean visible solar-disc fraction, longest darkness, time with some Earth visible and the longest Earth-visibility gap. Mean illumination uses hourly samples; events are searched at five-minute steps and boundaries refined. Shorter events may be missed, and boundary-touching intervals are labeled as lower bounds.

Follow the source trail.

Seven additional planets provide visual context, using documented system barycentre proxies where applicable. They do not change mission results, and their positions have not been independently checked against Horizons.

  1. NAIF SPICE positions and orientations.
  2. LRO/LOLA PDS and PGDA terrain elevation.
  3. Local terrain horizons and visible-disc geometry.
  4. Comparable windows, assumptions and executed validation evidence.

Selected angular results are checked against JPL Horizons, an official challenge resource. Full product versions, conventions, checksums and test records accompany the dataset.

Moon appearance: NASA’s Scientific Visualization Studio / LROC CGI Moon Kit. Earth appearance: NASA GSFC / Reto Stockli Blue Marble, a historical 2004 composite. LOLA displacement and local terrain supply explanatory real-scale geometry. NASA SVS/Gaia catalogue stars have illustrative brightness and colour. Body radii and distances are to physical scale; camera presets change viewpoint only; the surface sky uses apparent angular sizes and a terrain-horizon silhouette.

Limitations, rights and AI assistance

Earth visibility is not an operational radio link, DSN schedule or relay model. Sunlight is not electrical power or battery survival. The globe's day/night shading is distinct from numerical terrain visibility. This educational prototype does not certify landing safety or claim NASA endorsement.

Original code is Apache-2.0. Third-party data and software retain their own terms; source links and notices are public.
