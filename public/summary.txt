LUNAR NAVIGATOR — PROJECT SUMMARY
Team An0r4k | NASA Space Apps 2026
Challenge: Lunar Landing Site Navigator

THE QUESTION
If a lunar landing is delayed, does the same location still offer a useful window of sunlight and direct Earth visibility? Lunar Navigator makes that question explorable for non-specialists and inspectable for reviewers.

THE EXPERIENCE
The Guided analysis is designed as a roughly ten-minute investigation: arrive, read the terrain horizon, compare light and Earth visibility, test a delay and explain a decision. Chapters can be revisited or skipped. The Open Workspace exposes every screen immediately for a short review. No login or mission completion is required.

Home, How it works, Guided analysis, Simulator, Compare, Evidence and Credits are separate screens. The interactive guide contains seven lessons with adjustable examples, quizzes and a manual walkthrough; a ten-step tour points to real application controls. The simulator includes an immersive Explore space action that hides the interface, with a visible return control and Escape support.

TWO SIMULATION MODES
Faithful Mode uses one astronomical timestamp for all bodies. Free Mode lets the visitor hold selected positions or orientations fixed, making artificial experiments possible. Those configurations are explicitly labeled and do not change faithful mission results.

WHAT IS COMPARED
The prototype covers six points in published polar study areas: Connecting ridge, Shackleton rim, Nobile rim 1, Malapert massif and two nearby points selected by fixed offsets of about 1 km. They are not certified landing sites. The dataset spans 1 March to 28 April 2027, at observer heights of 0.5, 1, 2, 5 and 10 metres. Visitors compare an original arrival, a delayed arrival and an alternative point using the same operating duration and assumptions.

Outputs include mean visible solar-disc fraction, periods of direct sunlight, the longest continuous darkness, the fraction of time with some part of Earth visible and the longest gap in that visibility. Mean illumination uses hourly samples; event searches use five-minute steps and refined boundaries. Shorter events can be missed. Intervals touching a window boundary are marked as lower bounds.

DATA AND METHOD
NAIF SPICE supplies positions and orientations. Published LRO/LOLA PDS and PGDA elevation products supply terrain horizons. Selected angular results are checked against JPL Horizons, an official challenge resource. Dataset provenance, conventions, checksums and executed validation results are available in the Evidence screen and scientific documentation.

Seven additional planets follow documented ephemerides as visual context only, with system barycentre proxies where applicable. Their positions are not independently validated against Horizons and do not change the mission results.

The 3D globe uses credited NASA SVS/LROC imagery and real-scale LOLA displacement with polar filtering and physical surface normals. Named camera presets include the south pole, a surface sky, oblique local terrain and solar context. The 20 km local terrain windows are visual geometry at 40 m resolution. Catalogue-based stars use NASA SVS/Gaia data, with illustrative brightness and colour. All 3D body radii and distances share one physical scale; camera presets change viewpoint only. The surface sky uses true apparent angular sizes, a numerical terrain horizon and an optional real-DEM foreground aligned at the observer. Foreground fragments above the numerical horizon are discarded. The 40 m visualization does not model individual rocks; grain and brightness are illustrative. Global day/night shading is distinct from terrain visibility. Earth appearance is a historical NASA Blue Marble composite, not current weather.

LIMITS
Geometric Earth visibility is not an operational communications link, DSN schedule or relay model. Sunlight is not electrical power or battery survival. The project is an educational research prototype and does not certify landing safety or claim NASA endorsement.

OPEN SOURCE AND TRANSPARENCY
Original code is Apache-2.0. Third-party software and data retain their own terms. Credits, GitHub source links, preserved license notices and AI-use metadata are public. Claude and Codex assisted research, implementation, explanation and verification; numerical outputs are computed from the cited data rather than invented by language models. TRUE-SCALE SIMULATOR REVISION
The primary entry opens a ready-to-use landing-delay comparison; the ephemeris-driven simulator is available directly as a second option. No 3D planetary body has an enlarged radius or compressed distance. Camera inspection is distinct from physical size. Earth has credited 4k NASA ground imagery, a fixed illustrative cloud layer and ocean specular mask attributed to Solar System Scope (CC BY 4.0); these do not describe current weather. Solar glare and atmosphere are inexpensive optical illustrations. Scene visibility is computed from the same interpolated positions and terrain profile as the displayed time; the comparison timelines retain documented hourly source samples and refined events. Free experiments remain artificial and do not show mission metrics on the scene.

REVIEW AND REPRODUCIBILITY
A three-minute Review screen connects the problem, your current scenario results and executed evidence, without blocking any tool. Compare visualizes a user-defined darkness limit with lower-bound-aware feedback. Download its JSON to retain UTC windows, numerical summaries, selected coordinates, source provenance, derived-file checksums, build identity and limitations. Each metric links to its own explanation. Ideas from 24 previous global winners are summarised below.

NEW IN THIS RELEASE CANDIDATE
- Next transitions: the simulator lists the next sunrise, sunset, earthrise and earthset above the terrain horizon after the displayed instant, read from refined events (5-minute search, at most 1-minute refinement). No transition left in the period is stated as such, never as "never".
- Arrival skies: Compare shows the original and the delayed arrival sky side by side at the same point and height.
- Arrival-date series: for the selected point, every arrival date that fits the computed period, with the same calculation as the comparison table and visible lower bounds. It shows variation; it does not rank dates or points.
- Worked example with real data: at Malapert massif, raising the observer from 1 m to 5 m shortens the longest period without direct Sun from 289 h to 144 h over 1 March to 28 April 2027 (dataset values, 5-minute grid).
- Text description: in Faithful Mode, a polite screen-reader description of the Sun and Earth relative to the terrain horizon, updated when time stops, from the same interpolated geometry as the on-screen percentages.
- Product identifiers beside metrics, a plain-language negative control (a permanently shadowed pixel receives no direct Sun), and CI checks of derived-data hashes.

CLARITY AND PLAYBACK UPDATE
The home page includes a real two-arrival experiment: change the delay and see sunlight and Earth-visibility time immediately. The opening leads to the moving simulator; the comparison remains one click away. Three plain-language answers precede the optional full table and methods. Inspection in 3D preserves the original comparison. Surface sky now offers a real-terrain visualization and an optional horizon diagram; grey in the diagram means blocked sky, not unlit ground. A separate Local terrain view shows real-elevation geometry under the selected Sun direction. Play at the end now becomes Replay from start; Restart returns to the beginning paused. Five heights were computed independently; 0.5 m is particularly sensitive to unresolved terrain. Additional winner research and applied lessons: docs/CLARITY-CORRECTIONS.md.

SPACE MOTION, SURFACE AND PLAYBACK
Original, skippable spacecraft-to-logo opening; finite page/text entrances; a controllable four-step 2D tutorial; and inspection five minutes before/after calculated rise/set events. Reduced motion skips the automatic opening; the tools remain accessible directly. Play uses display-refresh rendering independently of slower text updates, capped at 60 FPS on mobile and 120 on desktop. These are ceilings, not promised hardware performance. HUD readings identify their actual rendered-frame time and remain present between clock updates. Optional light paths are explanatory diagrams, not visible vacuum beams or a ray-tracing calculation; optical glare and detailed shadows affect appearance only.

Twenty additional distinct global winners (2016-2019) were compared with this project. Their source evidence, applicable gaps, applied mechanisms and rejected out-of-scope features are recorded in docs/WINNER-GAP-20.md. No competitor code or assets were copied. Actual beginner comprehension remains an unmeasured human-validation task.

FIRST-MINUTE UPDATE
Normal-motion simulator entries start playing; reduced-motion, guided tours and exact comparison inspections start paused. Look at Sun and Look at Earth continuously follow their current apparent direction. Dragging or directional controls release following; zoom keeps it. Six calculated points include two nearby selections offset by about 1 km. Compare can fill a nearby alternative in one click while preserving the original date, height and stay.

VISION AND OPENING CONTROLS
Keep following Sun can be switched on or off. Enhanced vision optionally outlines the lunar limb, the calculated observer horizon or the local terrain-window boundary. It is illustrative and does not change light, physical radii or visibility measurements. The skippable opening appears on each full page visit/reload, without relying on cookies. Navigation within the app does not replay it; deep links are preserved and reduced-motion visitors skip the automatic opening.
